How it works

The derivative is built rule by rule: the sum rule, the product and quotient rules, the chain rule and the table of standard derivatives (powers, exponentials, logarithms, trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions). Each rule that fires becomes a step on the Steps tab, so you can follow the working the way a teacher would write it.

For awkward forms such as xˣ the tool uses logarithmic differentiation. Give a value in "Evaluate / tangent line at" to get f′(a) and the equation of the tangent line, and the plot shows the function, its derivative and the tangent together.

How the answer is checked

The symbolic answer is compared with a numerical derivative of your function (central differences) at several sample points. The result says so when they agree, and warns you when they do not.

Typing your input

  • Differentiate any expression. Type an equation in x and y (such as x^2 + y^2 = 25) to differentiate it implicitly.
  • Write powers with ^ (x^2), products with * or a space (2x, x sin(x)), and use ln for the natural logarithm.
  • Use the Variable option to differentiate with respect to t, y or any other letter; other letters are treated as constants (a partial derivative).
  • Order can be anything from 1 to 10.

Questions

Does it show the steps?

Yes. Every rule the tool applies (product, quotient, chain, power and the standard derivatives) is listed in order on the Steps tab, with the expression after each step.

How do I find the derivative at a point?

Type the point in "Evaluate / tangent line at", for example 2 or pi/4. You get f′(a), f(a) and the equation of the tangent line.

Can it do implicit differentiation?

Yes. Type an equation in x and y, such as x^2 + y^2 = 25, and you get dy/dx. See the implicit differentiation calculator for details.
