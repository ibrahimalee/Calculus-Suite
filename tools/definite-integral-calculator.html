How it works

The tool finds an antiderivative and applies the fundamental theorem of calculus. Infinite limits and singular endpoints are handled as limits; integrals that grow without bound are reported as divergent.

When no antiderivative exists in closed form, adaptive numerical integration (tanh-sinh and Gauss–Legendre) gives the value to many digits.

How the answer is checked

The exact value is compared with an independent numerical integration, and divergence is confirmed by watching the partial integrals grow.

Typing your input

  • Leave both limits empty for an indefinite integral. Limits accept pi, e and inf.
  • Use inf for ∞, for example lower 1 and upper inf.
  • pi, e and expressions such as pi/2 are allowed as limits.

Questions

Can it tell whether an improper integral converges?

Yes. Integrals such as ∫₁^∞ 1/x dx are reported as divergent, and convergent ones such as ∫₀¹ 1/√x dx get their value.

Does it give exact answers?

Where the antiderivative is known, yes, and common constants are recognised in the numerical value, for example √π for the Gaussian integral.
