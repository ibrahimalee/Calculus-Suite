How it works

Choose an operation and type f(x, y) or f(x, y, z). The gradient comes with the tangent plane at a point; the Hessian is classified by its eigenvalues to tell minima, maxima and saddle points apart; double integrals accept limits that depend on the other variable.

The 3D plot shows the surface or a contour map, and critical points are marked on it.

How the answer is checked

Derivatives are compared with finite differences, critical points are substituted back into the gradient, and double integrals are computed a second way numerically.

Typing your input

  • Separate several functions or vector-field components with ;
  • List the variables in the Variables option if they are not x, y, z.
  • Give a point as 1, 0 or 1, 0, 2.

Questions

How do I get a partial derivative with respect to y?

Choose the gradient or partial derivative operation, or in the Derivative tool set Variable to y. All other letters are held constant.

Can it find and classify critical points?

Yes. It solves ∇f = 0, then uses the Hessian at each point to label it a local minimum, local maximum or saddle point, and flags cases the test cannot decide.
