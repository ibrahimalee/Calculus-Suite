How to find a derivative

A derivative tells you how fast a function changes. Almost every derivative you will meet is built from a handful of rules, so the skill is knowing which rule to reach for.

Start with the standard derivatives

Memorise a short table: the derivative of xⁿ is n·xⁿ⁻¹, of eˣ is eˣ, of ln x is 1/x, of sin x is cos x and of cos x is −sin x. Everything else is a combination of these.

dxd​xn=nxn−1

The sum and constant-multiple rules

Differentiate term by term, and pull constants out front. The derivative of 3x² + 5x is 6x + 5.

The product rule

When two functions are multiplied, take the derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times the derivative of the second.

(uv)′=u′v+uv′

Example. Differentiate x² sin x: u = x², v = sin x

x2⋅sin(x)
f′(x)=2⋅x⋅sin(x)+x2⋅cos(x)

The quotient rule

For a fraction, the derivative is (u′v − uv′) divided by v². Check the sign of the numerator: it is the derivative of the top times the bottom, minus the top times the derivative of the bottom.

(vu​)′=v2u′v−uv′​

The chain rule

When one function sits inside another, differentiate the outside, leave the inside alone, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

dxd​f(g(x))=f′(g(x))g′(x)

Example. Differentiate sin(x²)

sin(x2)
f′(x)=2⋅x⋅cos(x2)

Check your answer

A quick check is to compare your formula with the slope of the graph at one or two points. The calculator does this automatically: every derivative is compared with a numerical derivative before it is shown.

Questions

Which rule do I use first?

Look at the outermost operation. If it is a product use the product rule, if it is a fraction the quotient rule, and if it is a function of a function the chain rule. Inner pieces are handled the same way.

Can I check my own working?

Yes. Type the function into the derivative calculator and compare each step with yours.
