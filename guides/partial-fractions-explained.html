Partial fractions, explained

A fraction like 1/(x² − 1) is awkward to integrate, but as a sum of two simple fractions it is easy. Partial fractions is the way to get there.

Before you start

The degree of the numerator must be smaller than the degree of the denominator. If it is not, divide first and work with the remainder.

Factor the denominator

Each distinct linear factor gets a term A/(x − a). A repeated factor (x − a)² gets two terms, A/(x − a) + B/(x − a)². An irreducible quadratic gets a numerator Bx + C.

Then integrate

Questions

How do I find A, B and C?

Multiply through by the denominator and either compare coefficients or substitute convenient values of x. The calculator checks the result by adding the pieces back together.
