Implicit differentiation, explained

Some curves, like the circle x² + y² = 25, cannot be written as a single y = f(x). Implicit differentiation finds the slope anyway, without solving for y.

The three steps

Differentiate both sides with respect to x. Every time you differentiate a term containing y, multiply by dy/dx (this is the chain rule). Then collect the dy/dx terms on one side and solve.

Example: the circle

For x² + y² = 25 you get 2x + 2y·y′ = 0, so y′ = −x/y. At the point (3, 4) the slope is −3/4.

Example. dy/dx for x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4)

x2+y2=25
dxdy​=y−x​

✓ Checked: solving the equation numerically for y just either side of 1 point on the curve gives the same slope as the formula (largest relative gap 3.3e-9).

Example: a curve with a product

When x and y are multiplied, the product rule applies as well. The folium x³ + y³ = 6xy is the classic case.

Example. dy/dx for x³ + y³ = 6xy at (3, 3)

x3+y3=6⋅x⋅y
dxdy​=−2⋅x+y22⋅y−x2​

✓ Checked: solving the equation numerically for y just either side of 1 point on the curve gives the same slope as the formula (largest relative gap 6.4e-7).

When the slope is undefined

If the denominator of dy/dx is zero at a point, the tangent there is vertical, or the curve has a corner or crossing.

Questions

Do I need to solve for y first?

No, that is the point of the method. Sometimes it is not even possible.

How do I find the second derivative?

Differentiate dy/dx again, replacing each y′ by the first result. The calculator shows this when you ask for order 2.
