Eigenvalues and eigenvectors, explained

An eigenvector of a matrix is a direction the matrix only stretches, never turns. The stretch factor is the eigenvalue.

The definition

A vector v is an eigenvector of A with eigenvalue λ if multiplying by A gives the same vector scaled by λ.

Av=λv

Finding eigenvalues

Rewrite the equation as (A − λI)v = 0. This has a non-zero solution only when the determinant of A − λI is zero. That determinant is a polynomial in λ called the characteristic polynomial.

Finding eigenvectors

For each eigenvalue, solve (A − λI)v = 0. The solutions form a line (or a plane, when the eigenvalue repeats).

A check you can do by hand

The sum of the eigenvalues equals the trace of the matrix, and their product equals the determinant.

Example. Determinant of the same matrix: 3 = 1 × 3

detA=3

A rotation has no real direction that stays put, so its eigenvalues are complex.

Questions

What if the eigenvalues repeat?

Then there may be fewer independent eigenvectors than you expect. The matrix [[1, 1], [0, 1]] has the eigenvalue 1 twice but only one eigenvector direction.
