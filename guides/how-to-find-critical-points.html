How to find critical points and extrema

Critical points are where a function stops rising or falling, so they are where maxima and minima hide. The derivative finds them, and the second derivative tells you which is which.

Step 1: solve f′(x) = 0

Critical points occur where the derivative is zero or does not exist. Solve f′(x) = 0 for x.

Step 2: classify with the second derivative

If f″ is positive at the point, the graph curves upward and it is a local minimum. If f″ is negative it is a local maximum. If f″ is zero the test is inconclusive and you look at the sign of f′ on both sides.

Example

For f(x) = x³ − 3x we get f′ = 3x² − 3, zero at x = ±1, and f″ = 6x, which is negative at −1 and positive at 1.

Example. Analysis of x³ − 3x

x3−3 x
Roots (f = 0): -1.732051, 0, 1.732051
Local maximum: (-1, 2)
Local minimum: (1, -2)

Inflection points

Where f″ changes sign the concavity switches. These are inflection points, which can be found in the same analysis.

Functions of two variables

Set both partial derivatives to zero and classify with the Hessian: positive definite means a minimum, negative definite a maximum, and mixed signs a saddle.

Example. Critical points of x³ − 3x + y²

x3−3⋅x+y2
Saddle point: (-1, 0), f = 2
Local minimum: (1, 0), f = -2

Questions

Is a critical point always an extremum?

No. f(x) = x³ has f′(0) = 0 but no maximum or minimum there.
