Taylor series, explained

A Taylor series approximates a function by a polynomial that matches its value and derivatives at one point. It is how calculators evaluate sin and eˣ.

The formula

The coefficient of (x − a)ⁿ is the n-th derivative at a, divided by n!.

f(x)≈n=0∑N​n!f(n)(a)​(x−a)n

Maclaurin series

When the centre is a = 0 the series is called a Maclaurin series. The ones worth knowing by heart are eˣ, sin x, cos x, 1/(1 − x) and ln(1 + x).

How good is it?

The polynomial is best near the centre and gets worse further away. The plot in the calculator overlays successive partial sums so you can see this.

Radius of convergence

Some series only converge on an interval. The series for 1/(1 − x) converges only for |x| < 1.
