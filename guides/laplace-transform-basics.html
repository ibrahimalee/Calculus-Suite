Laplace transform basics

The Laplace transform turns calculus problems in t into algebra problems in s. It is the standard tool for linear differential equations in engineering.

Definition

The transform multiplies f(t) by e^(−st) and integrates from 0 to infinity.

F(s)=∫0∞​f(t)e−stdt

A small table

Constants go to 1/s, t to 1/s², and eᵃᵗ to 1/(s − a). Sines and cosines give denominators of the form s² + ω².

Example. Transform of t²·e^(−3t)

t2⋅e−3 t
F(s)=(s+3)32​

Shifting

Multiplying by eᵃᵗ shifts s by a. That is why t²·e^(−3t) has (s + 3) in the denominator.

Going back

To invert, split F(s) into partial fractions and read each term off the table.

Example. Inverse transform of (s + 3)/(s² + 2s + 5)

s2+2 s+5s+3​
f(t)=exp(−t)⋅(cos(2⋅t)+sin(2⋅t));(t≥0)

✓ Checked: ∫₀^∞ f(t)e^(−st) dt reproduces F(s) at s = 1.9.

Differential equations

Transforming y″ + 3y′ + 2y = 0 gives an algebraic equation for Y(s), which you solve and invert.

Example. Solve y″ + 3y′ + 2y = 0, y(0) = 1, y′(0) = 0

y(t)=2⋅exp(−t)−exp(−(2⋅t))

✓ Checked: substituting y(t) back satisfies the equation and the initial values (max residual 2.2e-16).

Questions

Why use Laplace transforms?

They turn derivatives into multiplication by s, which makes linear differential equations with initial conditions algebraic.
