Second derivative of x² sin(x)

dx2d2​[x2⋅sin(x)]

Answer

f′′(x)=2⋅sin(x)+4⋅x⋅cos(x)−x2⋅sin(x)

✓ Checked: agrees with an independent power-series computation at 5 sample points.

Open this in the calculatorDerivative calculator with steps

Step by step

  1. Differentiate (order 1)

    d/dx [ x^2 * sin(x) ] = 2 * x * sin(x) + x^2 * cos(x)

    dxd​[x2⋅sin(x)]=2⋅x⋅sin(x)+x2⋅cos(x)
  2. Differentiate (order 2)

    d/dx [ 2 * x * sin(x) + x^2 * cos(x) ] = 2 * sin(x) + 4 * x * cos(x) - x^2 * sin(x)

    dxd​[2⋅x⋅sin(x)+x2⋅cos(x)]=2⋅sin(x)+4⋅x⋅cos(x)−x2⋅sin(x)
  3. Simplify

    Result: 2 * sin(x) + 4 * x * cos(x) - x^2 * sin(x)

    2⋅sin(x)+4⋅x⋅cos(x)−x2⋅sin(x)

About this kind of problem

A second derivative is the derivative of the derivative. It tells you about concavity: where it is positive the graph curves upward, where it is negative it curves downward.

For this problem the working uses: Differentiate (order 1), Differentiate (order 2), Simplify.
