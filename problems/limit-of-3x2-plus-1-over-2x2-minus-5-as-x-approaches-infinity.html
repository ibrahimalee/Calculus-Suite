Limit of (3x^2+1)/(2x^2-5) as x approaches infinity

  1. Direct substitution gives an indeterminate form

    Numerator and denominator both tend to ±∞: form ∞/∞.

    x→∞lim​2 x2−53 x2+1​;→;form ∞∞​
  2. L'Hôpital's rule (round 1)

    Differentiate numerator and denominator separately: (3 x^2 + 1)′ = 6 * x, (2 x^2 - 5)′ = 4 * x.

  4. L'Hôpital's rule (round 2)

    Differentiate numerator and denominator separately: (6 * x)′ = 6, (4 * x)′ = 4.

    x→∞lim​4⋅x6⋅x​=x→∞lim​46​

    The limit of the last quotient is 3/2.

    x→∞lim​2 x2−53 x2+1​=23​

About this kind of problem

A limit describes what a function approaches near a point or at infinity. Indeterminate forms such as 0/0 are worked through with L'Hôpital's rule, and the left and right approaches are compared so jumps and infinite limits are not missed.

For this problem the working uses: Direct substitution gives an indeterminate form, L'Hôpital's rule (round 1), Still indeterminate, L'Hôpital's rule (round 2), Evaluate the new limit, Evaluate for large |x|.
