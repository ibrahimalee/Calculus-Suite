Limit of sin(x)/x as x approaches 0

x→0lim​xsin(x)​

Answer

limx→0​xsin(x)​=1
  • Left (x → 0⁻): 1
  • Right (x → 0⁺): 1

Step by step

  1. Direct substitution gives an indeterminate form

    Numerator and denominator both tend to 0: form 0/0.

    x→0lim​xsin(x)​;→;form 00​
  2. L'Hôpital's rule (round 1)

    Differentiate numerator and denominator separately: (sin(x))′ = cos(x), (x)′ = 1.

    x→0lim​xsin(x)​=x→0lim​1cos(x)​
  3. Evaluate the new limit

    The limit of the last quotient is 1.

    x→0lim​xsin(x)​=1
  4. Approach from both sides

    Evaluate f at x = 0 ± 10⁻², 10⁻³, … 10⁻⁹.

  5. Compare

    Left → 1, right → 1.

About this kind of problem

A limit describes what a function approaches near a point or at infinity. Indeterminate forms such as 0/0 are worked through with L'Hôpital's rule, and the left and right approaches are compared so jumps and infinite limits are not missed.

For this problem the working uses: Direct substitution gives an indeterminate form, L'Hôpital's rule (round 1), Evaluate the new limit, Approach from both sides, Compare.
