Integral of sin(2x)

∫sin(2 x)dx

Answer

∫sin(2 x)dx=2−1​⋅cos(2⋅x)+C

✓ Checked: the antiderivative was checked by differentiating it at sample points.

Open this in the calculatorIntegral calculator with steps

Step by step

  1. Worked solution (U-substitution)

    The steps below show one valid technique; every antiderivative agrees up to the constant C.

  2. u-substitution

    Let u = 2 x, so du = 2 dx.

    u=2 x,du=2dx
  3. Rewrite in terms of u

    The integral becomes ∫ sin(u) / 2 du.

    ∫2sin(u)​du
  4. Integrate

    ∫ sin(u) / 2 du = cos(u) * -1 / 2

    2cos(u)⋅−1​
  5. Substitute back

    Replace u with 2 x.

    2cos(2 x)⋅−1​
  6. Assemble the result

    F(x) = cos(2 x) * -1 / 2

    F(x)=2cos(2 x)⋅−1​
  7. Add the constant of integration

    Every antiderivative differs by a constant, so we add + C.

    2−1​⋅cos(2⋅x)+C
  8. Check by differentiating

    d/dx [ -1 / 2 * cos(2 * x) ] = sin(2 * x), which matches the integrand.

    dxd​[2−1​⋅cos(2⋅x)]=sin(2⋅x)

About this kind of problem

An antiderivative is a function whose derivative is the one you were given. The working names the technique (table, substitution, by parts or partial fractions) and every answer is differentiated back to confirm it.

For this problem the working uses: Worked solution (U-substitution), u-substitution, Rewrite in terms of u, Integrate, Substitute back, Assemble the result.
