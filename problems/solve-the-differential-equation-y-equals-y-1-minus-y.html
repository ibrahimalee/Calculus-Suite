Solve the differential equation y′ = y·(1-y)

y′=y⋅(1−y)

Answer

dxdy​=y⋅(1−y)
  • Implicit solution: log(abs(y)) - log(abs(y - 1)) = x + C

Open this in the calculatorDifferential equation solver

Step by step

  1. Recognise a separable ODE

    y′ = g(x)·h(y) with g = 1, h = y - y^2.

    dxdy​=(1)(y−y2)
  2. Separate the variables

    dy / (y - y^2) = (1) dx

    y−y2dy​=(1)dx
  3. Integrate both sides

    log(abs(y)) - log(abs(y - 1)) = x + C

    ln(∣y∣)−ln(∣y−1∣)=x+C
  4. Note

    Constant solutions y = y₀ with h(y₀) = 0 may also exist and are not covered by the separated form.

  5. Solution values

    y(0) ≈ 1 y(0.75) ≈ 1 y(1.5) ≈ 1 y(2.25) ≈ 1 y(3) ≈ 1

About this kind of problem

First-order equations are solved in closed form when they are linear or separable, with the integrating factor or the separation shown, and the solution is substituted back into the equation.

For this problem the working uses: Recognise a separable ODE, Separate the variables, Integrate both sides, Note, Solution values.
